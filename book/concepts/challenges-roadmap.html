Challenges & Translational Roadmap

Source: §7–8 of the paper

Challenge 1 — Chemical complexity & mechanism analysis

  • Herbal material composition varies with geographic origin, harvest season, and processing (drying, steaming, stir-frying); ginsenoside/astragaloside levels can vary severalfold → batch-to-batch discrepancies underlie conflicting efficacy reports. Fix: chromatographic fingerprinting + good agricultural/manufacturing practices.
  • Different aging models (D-galactose-induced, naturally aged, SAMP8) recapitulate different aspects of aging → differential sensitivity to a given formula.
  • Species differences in drug-metabolizing enzymes/target homology → multi-species validation required.
  • Contrast with synthetic multi-target drugs: high-affinity synthetic agents (AMPK activators/mTOR inhibitors, PI3K/mTOR dual inhibitors) suffer off-target kinase binding, feedback-driven compensatory activation, cascading toxicities (GI/metabolic). TCM formulas work the opposite way: low plasma concentration + modest target affinity + multi-component synergy → "multi-target fine-tuning" that rebalances the network without triggering compensatory activation, plus organ-preference that reduces off-target risk.
  • Future: systematically integrate AI + network pharmacology + multi-omics to build a "components–targets–aging-phenotype" regulatory map, and prioritize proteomic aging-clock quantification of formula effects on organ biological age.

Challenge 2 — Clinical evidence gap

  • Current TCM anti-aging clinical studies: small samples, subjective endpoints, rarely use internationally recognized aging biomarkers (epigenetic clocks; circulating pro-aging proteins GAS6, GPNMB).
  • Breakthrough path — biomarker-guided trials:
    • Enrich trial populations via blood-proteomic signatures or TCM constitution typing.
    • Use quantitative endpoints: epigenetic age reversal, reduced SA-β-gal positivity rate → shorter trials, higher evidence level, faster regulatory registration.

Challenge 3 — New-drug conversion barriers

  • Promising formulas stuck in basic research. Obstacles: poor batch consistency, no standardized "drug-efficacy substance list," missing long-term safety/drug-interaction data, unclear product positioning (drug vs functional food).
  • Roadmap — "chemical QC → mechanism verification → clinical evaluation":
    1. Chemical level: high-resolution mass spec → prescription fingerprint spectra for quality control.
    2. Mechanism level: multi-level models verify locking-effect mechanisms and active component groups.
    3. Clinical level: accumulate clinical data for medicine-and-food homologous formulas via functional-food routes (safer); for formulas with clear therapeutic effects, run RCTs with aging biomarkers as alternative endpoints.

Conclusion (paper §8, distilled)

The review establishes an integrative model fusing TCM principles with modern aging biology (14 hallmarks), providing a translational research foundation for healthy longevity: theory mapping (§3), formula-mechanism catalog (§4), constituent/synergy chemistry (§5), AI/multi-omics methods (§6), and a concrete translation path (§7).
