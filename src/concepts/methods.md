# Methodological Innovations

*Source: [§6 of the paper](../sources/pmc-13620404-fulltext.md)*

Three methodological pillars advance TCM anti-aging research:

## 1. Network pharmacology + AI-driven target prediction

- Network pharmacology + molecular docking: systematic prediction of active ingredients, target identification, pathway enrichment.
- Deep integration with AI/ML: integrate databases (e.g., TCMSP) with protein-interaction networks → predict aging-related signalling nodes (AMPK, TLR7/8) for formulas like BYHWD.
- ML-based high-throughput **senolytic screening** over large datasets (including TCM banks): identified **614 potential senolytic compounds** and **81 herbs** with senolytic characteristics.
- Known limitations: training-set bias toward well-characterized compounds; ML models are optimized for single compounds and struggle to capture multi-component synergy. Proposed fix: pair AI screening with **molecular docking** against aging-relevant proteins (SIRT1, mTOR, NF-κB) and validate in vitro/in vivo.

## 2. Multi-omics integration + aging-clock assessment

- Transcriptomics, 16S rRNA sequencing, untargeted metabolomics: reveal pathway enrichment, energy/amino-acid metabolic correction, key protein targets.
- **Proteomic aging clocks** (ultra-high-sensitivity mass spectrometry + ML): evaluate biological age across 12 tissue types; quantitatively score how a formula reverses multi-organ biological age (e.g., BZBC reversed DNA-methylation age in mice).
- High-resolution analytical chemistry (UPLC-Q-TOF-MS, UPLC-Q-Orbitrap MS/MS) identifies and characterizes complex formula components → standardized quantitative efficacy evaluation.

## 3. Multi-level model biovalidation system

A validation ladder across biological complexity:

| Level | Model | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *C. elegans* | Lifespan, stress resistance, genetic pathway validation (e.g., ZGP/YGP autophagy; DAF-16/HSF-1/SKN-1) |
| 2 | *Drosophila* | Intermediate validation of complex behavioural/physiological assessments |
| 3 | Rodents | D-galactose-induced aging (organ-specific effects); **SAMP8** rapid-aging mice for brain aging/cognition |
| 4 | Natural aging models | Most clinically relevant; longer timelines |
| 5 | Humans | RCTs |

Cross-species consistency (e.g., BZBC showing effects in *C. elegans*, mice, and humans) strengthens evidence far beyond any single model — cross-species validation is a methodological cornerstone for translation.
