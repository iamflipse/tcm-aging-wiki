# Mapping TCM Onto the 14 Hallmarks of Aging

*Source: [§3.2–3.3 of the paper](../sources/pmc-13620404-fulltext.md)*

## The 14 hallmarks of aging

Aging is conceptualized as a process driven by interconnected molecular features, articulated and expanded by **López-Otín and colleagues**. The 14 hallmarks:

1. Genomic instability
2. Telomere attrition
3. Epigenetic alterations
4. Loss of proteostasis
5. Disabled macroautophagy
6. Deregulated nutrient sensing
7. Mitochondrial dysfunction
8. Cellular senescence
9. Stem cell exhaustion
10. Altered intercellular communication
11. Chronic inflammation
12. Dysbiosis
13. Changes in the extracellular matrix
14. Social-psychological isolation

These interconnected hallmarks are core contributors to age-related functional decline across organ systems, and the paper argues they align with the TCM paradigm of "multi-component, multi-target, holistic regulation."

## Proteomic aging clocks (quantification tool)

- The **2025 proteomic aging atlas (Ding et al.)**: the most comprehensive human aging map to date — ultra-high-sensitivity mass spectrometry + machine learning, proteomic clocks across **12 tissue types** covering seven physiological systems.
- Findings: **age 30** = initial divergence point of aging trajectories (adrenal tissue shows the earliest features); **ages 45–55** = critical turning point (surge in differentially expressed proteins).
- Key circulating **pro-aging proteins: GAS6, GPNMB, COMP, HTRA1, IGFBP7** — directly induce vascular-cell senescence; shift aging research toward systemic inter-organ communication.
- A 2024 *Cell Metabolism* study applied ML to blood proteomics: protein signatures of monocyte senescence predicting frailty, inflammation, and metabolic dysfunction.
